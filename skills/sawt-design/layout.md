# Layout and composition (graphic design for a 1080×1920 reel)

General graphic-design principles, filtered for vertical video, Arabic, and this owner's taste.

## 1. Hierarchy: decide the order of reading first
- Before placing anything, write the reading order: **1** the hero (a face or one big word), **2** the supporting line,
  **3** anything small. If you can't rank it, there's too much on screen (Hick's law, principle 15).
- Hierarchy comes from **scale** first, then **weight**, **contrast/light**, **position**, **colour**, **timing**. Use two
  or three of these, not all. Colour alone never carries emphasis.
- **One hero per frame.** A frame with a face *and* a huge word: the word is placed so it frames the face (behind it,
  around it), never competing beside it.
- Size ratios that read on a phone: hero word 3–5× the supporting line (e.g. 180 px over 50 px). Small text never
  under ~40 px at 1080 wide.

## 2. Negative space is an active element
- Empty space is where the eye rests and where the hero gets its power. The owner's "premium" references all leave
  half the frame or more quiet. Our rejected frames were busy (cards, maps, props, labels at once).
- Don't fill a gap because it's there. A dark or blurred area of the photo is a place for one word, not a place for
  three elements.
- **Black (or deep brown) is a shot.** One word alone on the dark gives the next image force.

## 3. Grid, alignment, safe areas
- Work on a simple grid: thirds vertically (top / middle / bottom bands) and a centre axis. Most frames: the subject on
  the middle band or a third, the word on the opposite third.
- **Instagram safe area:** keep text and key faces inside roughly x 60–990, y 220–1600 (top ~220 px and bottom ~320 px
  and the right ~90 px are covered by the interface).
- Align things to each other (shared centre line, shared baseline). Random offsets read as accidents; deliberate large
  offsets read as design.
- Margins are generous and consistent within a film.

## 4. Gestalt: how the eye groups things
- **Proximity:** things close together read as one unit (a word and its sub-line). Separate units need clearly more
  space between them than inside them.
- **Similarity:** same font/colour = same role. Keep roles consistent through a film (all names in one style, all
  years in one style).
- **Continuity:** the eye follows lines and curves; use them (a ribbon, a route, an orbit) to lead to the hero.
- **Figure and ground:** the hero must separate from the background by light or blur, not by a box around it
  (over busy footage: a soft shadow or gradient scrim under the text).
- **Closure:** a partly hidden word (behind a head) is still read whole if two-thirds of it shows.

## 5. Depth (the most important missing piece)
The owner's verdict on our older work is "flat". Depth comes from layers:
- **Foreground / subject / background**, each moving at its own speed (parallax), with soft focus on the far layer.
- **Text placed between layers:** behind the subject's head, behind a building, a ribbon passing behind then in front
  (needs a cut-out; see `../motion-edit/camera-and-compositing.md`).
- **Light:** a soft key light on the subject, falloff to the edges (vignette), a faint glow on the hero word.
- **Shadow:** soft, consistent direction, subtle. Never a hard drop shadow.

## 6. Right to left
- Arabic reading order runs right → left and top → bottom: the first thing to read sits top-right; motion that
  introduces things usually travels right → left (a write-on reveals right → left).
- **Mirror** for RTL: reading order, the direction of "forward in time" on a horizontal timeline, progress lines.
- **Don't mirror:** clocks (still clockwise), photos (never flip a face or a historical photo), numbers (١٩٧٢ stays as
  is), logos, maps (north stays up, real geography). Media-like progress (a playhead) keeps its direction.
- Mixed Arabic and English: the Arabic is the hero, English small beneath; never set them side by side as equals.
Sources: [Material bidirectionality](https://m1.material.io/usability/bidirectionality.html),
[Wikimedia Codex](https://doc.wikimedia.org/codex/main/style-guide/bidirectionality.html).

## 7. Images
- Photos keep their true shape; never stretch or squash (the Warda flag "squashed both ways").
- Crop with intent: tight on the face for emotion, wide for place. Never let a crop or mask cut through a face.
- Straight, unframed prints in collages (the liked pop-up collage); no fake paper cards, tape and pins as decoration.
- Real objects and symbols (the real flag, real pyramids), never clip-art stand-ins.

## 8. Quick self-check on any still
1. Can I name the hero in one second? 2. Does the eye travel from it in the right order (right → left, top → down)?
3. Is at least a third of the frame quiet? 4. Is everything inside the safe area? 5. Does anything look stuck on top
instead of sitting in the scene? 6. Would this still work as a poster?

[Dribbble: principles of design](https://dribbble.com/resources/principles-of-design).
