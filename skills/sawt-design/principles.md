# The twenty principles (owner's list, 2026-11-17), with how each applies to our videos

Twelve are Disney's animation principles and eight are UX laws; for video the UX laws mean "how fast and easily a
viewer understands the screen". **Go through all twenty on the stills of every new scene before showing it**, and
say which ones a design breaks on purpose. (They were saved on 17 Nov and then not enforced; the owner's complaints
since, "kids' show", "flat", "basic", map almost one-to-one onto the ones that were skipped: marked ★.)

| # | Principle | What it means for a Sawt Al-Zaman scene | Failure we already made |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Squash and stretch | A landing word squashes 2–4 % and recovers; our Arabic version is the static kashida stretch (never animate the stretch). Subtle for this brand. | — |
| 2 | ★ Anticipation | A small move the opposite way before the main one: a word dips 6 px before it rises, the camera eases back before a push. Tells the eye where to look. | Words slid straight in |
| 3 | ★ Staging | One focus per moment. Light, size, contrast and empty space all point at it; everything else waits. | Map and name card frames, BaeedAnak v2 |
| 4 | Pose to pose | Design the key frames (posters) first, then the in-betweens. Our "stills before motion" rule. | — |
| 5 | ★ Follow-through, overlap | Parts don't stop together: the frame settles after the photo inside it, words of a line settle in turn, a ribbon keeps moving after the subject stops. | Everything stopped at once |
| 6 | Slow in, slow out | Every move eased; ease-out for arrivals, ease-in for exits. Nothing starts or stops at full speed. | — |
| 7 | ★ Arcs | Things travel on curves: camera across a board, a word flying in from off-screen, an orbit. | Straight-line slides |
| 8 | Secondary action | A small supporting motion (dust as a photo lands, a pen line under a title) that never steals focus. | — |
| 9 | ★ Timing | Snap into place (6–12 frames), then hold still long enough to read. Mix quick and calm. | Slow drifts that never landed |
| 10 | ★ Exaggeration | Push the one key moment a little past real so it reads on a phone. Only the key moment. | — |
| 11 | ★ Solid drawing | Weight, depth, light: consistent shadows, perspective, parallax between layers; text sits *in* the scene. | Flat text and props on top of the picture |
| 12 | ★ Appeal | Simple confident shapes, taste in type and colour, no clutter. | Candles, flowers, stick figures, oud strings |
| 13 | Fitts's law | The important thing is big and near where the eye already is; don't make the eye jump across the frame for the key word. | — |
| 14 | Bigger hit areas | Key elements generous in size; small things on a phone are missed. | Tiny captions |
| 15 | ★ Hick's law | Fewer elements at once = faster understanding. One thought at a time. | ~15 devices in 50 s (BaeedAnak v2) |
| 16 | Miller's law | Never more than a few things to read or track; chunk information. | — |
| 17 | Doherty threshold | Something answers the voice within ~0.4 s; the first frame already shows something. | — |
| 18 | Postel's law | Accept messy input (screen recordings, rough clips, dialects); always output clean, consistent video. | Audible music jumps (fixed since) |
| 19 | Jakob's law | Familiar visual language: Arabic reads right to left, captions sit low, reels read top to bottom. Break it only on purpose. | — |
| 20 | Frequency of use | Recurring brand elements (logo ending, caption style, palette) stay identical and instantly recognisable; rare things can be special. | — |

Related professional frameworks (same ideas, other words): Issara Willenskomer's *UX in Motion* (easing, offset and
delay, parenting, transformation; motion must carry meaning, not decoration), Material Design motion (duration scales
with distance; exits shorter than entrances; asymmetric easing feels natural), Apple's HIG (motion should be purposeful,
brief and realistic; gratuitous animation disorients). Sources: [UX Mag](https://uxmag.com/articles/creating-usability-with-motion-the-ux-in-motion-manifesto),
[Material](https://m1.material.io/motion/duration-easing.html), [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion).
