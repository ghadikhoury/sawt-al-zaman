# Texture and light: the finishing layer

The owner calls light and grain "obvious": a baseline every film must have, done quietly. It's what makes separate
layers (photos, cut-outs, type) feel like one filmed world instead of a slideshow.

## 1. The finishing order (each effect feeds the next)
1. **Grade** (repair → clarity → look; `colour-grading.md`).
2. **Light:** a soft key on the subject (dodge), falloff to the edges (vignette), **halation** only around real bright
   highlights (stage lights, jewellery): a warm reddish glow, separate from bloom.
3. **Bloom** (optional, very low): a soft glow from the brightest areas, for a lit sign or a lamp.
4. **Grain last**, so contrast and grading don't clip it; moving, strongest in the mid-tones, size matched to the source.
Getting the order wrong clips the grain, desaturates halation, or darkens dirt instead of the scene
([VSCO engineering: film FX, light and texture](https://eng.vsco.co/film-fx-part-1),
[StraySpark: halation, grain, gate weave](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/blender-film-look-halation-grain-gate-weave)).

## 2. Rules of restraint
- **Felt, not noticed.** If you see the grain or the vignette first, it's too strong.
- **Vignette can be off-centre**, darkest away from where the light comes from; it should lead the eye to the hero.
- **Light leaks / flashes:** blended with `screen` or `soft-light`, one or two in a film; more turns into a filter
  ([PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/video-editing-light-leaks-film-grain)).
- **No fake-vintage clichés:** heavy scratches, burn holes, full-strength sepia, constant jitter, VHS bleed.
- **Glow on type:** faint, warm, in brand colours (parchment/champagne), never neon.
- **Instagram eats grain:** check the uploaded result; slightly coarser grain survives compression better.

## 3. Light as a design tool
- Light defines the hero: the brightest area of the frame should be the face or the word.
- A slow light change can carry a story beat (a room getting darker at the illness, brighter at the return).
- Matching light between layers: a cut-out placed on a new background gets the background's light direction and
  colour (a soft shadow, a warm edge light), or it looks pasted.
